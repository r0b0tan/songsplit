Frequently asked questions

As of November 2026

1. What does MixApart do?

MixApart takes a song apart into separate stems, using AI. You choose an MP3 or WAV, wait a moment, and end up with several faders instead of one. How finely it is taken apart is up to you, through the separation mode:

  • Minimal makes two stems out of it, vocals and everything else together.
  • Fast makes four: vocals, drums, bass and the rest.
  • Instruments makes six and pulls out guitar and piano as well.

That is useful when you are learning an instrument. Drums out, play along yourself. Or listen to the vocals alone because you cannot make out the words. A difficult passage can be pulled down to half speed without sounding any lower. Every stem can be saved on its own.

All of it runs in your browser. No account, no sign-up, no queue. And your music stays on your device while it happens.

2. How does it work?

Behind it sits an AI model called htdemucs. A neural network that has learned from many thousands of songs what a singing voice sounds like, what drums sound like and what a bass sounds like. Not from fixed rules, but from examples — roughly the way image and speech AI is trained as well.

While separating, the model listens through your song in short sections and decides for every sound which stem it belongs to. Then it puts the sections back together.

htdemucs comes out of Meta's AI research and is freely available. It is among the best there is for this task, and it sits inside programs people pay for, too. So you are not getting a stripped down version here.

What is more interesting is where it runs. Models like this used to need a server with a powerful graphics card. MixApart runs them through a new browser interface called WebGPU, directly on the graphics card in your own device. That is why your music does not have to go anywhere for the separation.

3. When is my data where?

Normally your music stays on your device. The field on the start page looks like an upload, but it is not one. Your browser only opens the file, the way a music player would open it, and computes with it on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere, neither the file nor the finished stems.

So there is nothing of your music for MixApart to see or to store. After the first song this even works without an internet connection. You can switch on airplane mode and carry on separating.

There is one exception. Some devices cannot do the computing themselves, phones above all — more on that in question 9. For those, MixApart offers separation on a server, and then the file does leave your device. It goes to Modal Labs in the USA, a provider of computing time on graphics cards. The same model separates it there, and the stems come back. That is the difference from the field on the start page: there the file is only opened, here it is sent to a server.

This does not happen by itself. It happens when the header says "Server" and you tap "Separate stems". The finished stems are deleted there after a few hours; at the provider, the file and the result may sit for a few days. The exact periods and the legal details are in the privacy policy under section 3 (German).

Nothing is left behind once you close the page. The stems live only in the browser's memory. Tab closed, gone. Whatever you want to keep, save it beforehand with the arrow symbol on the respective stem.

The only thing that stays for good is the model itself, around 174 MB in the browser cache. That has nothing to do with your music; it only makes sure the second song starts right away.

4. May I do this with music I bought?

For your own use, yes. German copyright law allows private copies, and a stem you pull out of a song you bought in order to practise with it falls under that. Playing along, singing along, using it in the rehearsal room — all unproblematic. Rules differ from country to country, so if you are not in Germany, the details where you live may not be the same.

What you may not do is publish the stems. Putting them on YouTube, selling them as a karaoke version or building them into a recording of your own needs permission from the rights holders. The separation changes nothing about that; the material stays someone else's.

So the same applies as to any other music that is not yours. MixApart does not check this and cannot check it. If you need to know precisely, ask somebody qualified to advise you.

5. How do I get a backing track without vocals?

With the Minimal separation mode. It makes exactly two stems, the vocals and everything else. Mute the vocals and the backing track is done.

It works the other way round just as well. Mute everything except the vocals and you have the voice on its own, for instance to make out the words. Minimal is no slower than Fast, by the way, and it loads nothing extra — it only merges the stems afterwards.

6. Can I get guitar and piano separately too?

Yes, with the Instruments separation mode. It works with a larger model and delivers six stems instead of four, adding guitar and piano.

Two things are different about it. The model is larger, so the separation takes longer, and it is downloaded once the first time you use it. Also, this mode only exists when your device does the computing itself. Over the server it is always four stems.

7. What can the player do?

More than is visible at first glance. Every stem has its own volume fader and can be muted or heard on its own.

You can set the tempo between 50 and 125 percent without the music sounding any lower or higher. Pitch you shift separately from that, by up to twelve semitones, in case a song does not sit in your range or your guitar is tuned a semitone down.

For difficult passages there is a loop. You mark the start and the end, and the excerpt runs round and round. And when you listen to one stem on its own, that stem gets its own tempo while the rest carries on as normal.

8. Which devices and browsers work?

On a computer, Chrome and Edge; on a Mac, Safari as well. Firefox manages it on Windows and on newer Macs, on Linux not yet.

On iPhone and iPad it works from iOS 26 onwards, since autumn 2025. Because every browser there uses the same underpinnings, that applies to Safari, Chrome and Firefox alike. On Android phones it depends on the model — more on that in the next question.

Trying it is easier than looking it up. The page checks what your device can do when it opens, and tells you.

9. Why does the page say my device cannot do the computing?

An AI model of this size needs a graphics card, a GPU. The same one that otherwise draws games and videos. The main processor, the CPU, can do the same arithmetic in theory, only many times slower.

Browsers release the GPU for computations like this through WebGPU, but not everywhere yet. On Android phones only some of the chips in use have been enabled so far, and in some corporate environments the release is switched off altogether.

Nothing can be done about that from outside; the browser maker decides it for your device model. On a computer, a current graphics driver sometimes helps, or switching to a recent version of Chrome or Edge.

The server exists for exactly this case. It takes over the separation and sends the four stems back. You do not have to change anything for it: the page adjusts by itself, and in the player everything is as usual afterwards.

If you get in touch, please quote the short code from the message, GPU-03 for example. That makes it possible to tell immediately what the problem was.

10. How long does a separation take?

On a computer with a graphics card you are usually finished sooner than the song you are separating lasts. How much sooner depends on your hardware. That is why there is no number here. MixApart shows you one itself instead: while it separates, a remaining time runs along, and it comes from your own run.

Over the server, a whole song takes about a minute. On top of that comes the wait until a machine is free there.

The very first time takes longer, because the model is downloaded once. Every further song starts right away.

11. Which files can I use?

MP3 and WAV. Over the server it is at most 25 MB per file, which is enough for a good ten minutes of music. On your own device the memory decides how long a song may be, and the page asks before it gets tight.

12. Can I use the stems in my music software?

Yes. The arrow symbol on each stem saves it as WAV, 44.1 kHz in stereo, 16 bit. Every recording program copes with that, from Audacity to Ableton.

All the stems are the same length and start at the same point. So you can simply drag them into your project side by side, without aligning anything.

13. Does it cost anything? Do I need an account?

MixApart is free and usable without signing up. No account, no paywall, no limit on a certain number of songs. The app does not set advertising or analytics cookies either.

14. What do the words in the header mean?

They say what the computing is running on right now. The names are technical; what they mean is fairly simple:

  • WebGPU means your graphics card is doing the computing, in the browser. The usual route and the fastest one. Your file stays on your device.
  • WASM means your main processor is doing it, also in the browser. That works on any device but takes considerably longer, because a CPU is not built for this kind of arithmetic. Your music stays with you here as well.
  • Server means a graphics card in the USA is doing it. The route for devices that cannot manage on their own. For this, the file is transferred.
